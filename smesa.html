Executive summary

Our search traffic is falling, and the recent numbers show it clearly. Clicks from Google dropped 63% between January and May this year, and our average position slipped from #12 to #19. Three things are behind it: search itself is changing, we changed our own site on purpose, and people are getting answers from AI instead of clicking through.

The short version

So far in 2026 we've had 74,942 search clicks off 10,273,767 impressions. The trend inside that is the worry: every month this year has been lower than the one before, and May was our weakest yet at 9,852 clicks. We're still being shown to people — impressions are huge — but fewer of them click, because more of the answer now sits on the results page itself.

At the same time, some of the change is by choice. In February 2026 we moved from a resource platform that mostly collected leads to a membership platform. In four months we've signed up 2,364 members. One scoreboard went quiet on purpose while a new one started counting.

What is driving the decline

  1. Google now puts AI-written answers at the top of many results, so people read the answer without reaching our site — even when our article is the source.
  2. Our rankings have slipped: average position has drifted from #12 to nearly #19 this year, and high-value commercial keywords have fallen off the first pages.
  3. We changed the website. Moving content behind a membership wall and retiring resource pages changes what Google can index and what visitors can reach.
  4. We lean on too few channels, so any shift at Google hits us fast.

What is going well

  • The membership launch worked: 2,364 sign-ups in four months, 95% of our search audience is South African, and members span every province.
  • The funding story is our strength — our best pages, best emails and busiest community threads are all about access to money.
  • We still rank first on our core terms: sme funding, business advice and sme resources.

Section 03

The AI factor — why answers are replacing clicks

The biggest reason the clicks are falling while impressions stay high: AI now answers the question on the results page, so people don't need to click. There are two sides to this, and they pull in opposite sizes.

The traffic AI sends us — small, but growing

People who start in an assistant and click through to read more show up in our data. Over the past year ChatGPT, Perplexity, Copilot, Gemini and Claude sent us 23,707 sessions (2.0% of traffic). It barely existed eighteen months ago, and these visitors engage better than average.

The traffic AI keeps — large, and invisible

This is the real threat, and it shows up nowhere in an export. When Google posts an AI Overview, or someone asks ChatGPT "how do I register a company in South Africa", the assistant answers using content like ours and nobody clicks. We did the work; we get none of the visit. That's exactly what our own data shows — millions of impressions, a 0.7% click rate.

What we do about it
We can't stop this, but we can decide whether our content is the source it quotes — and whether that quote earns a click. That means writing what an AI can't fully answer: first-hand reporting, local detail, named sources, real South African examples. The next section is the brief for the writing team.

Section 04

Writing for humans in an AI world

If AI is going to read and summarise our articles, our writing has to do the one thing a machine can't fake: sound like a person who actually knows South African business. When our content reads like the average of everything online, an AI can reproduce it perfectly — so there's no reason to visit us. Distinctive, first-hand writing is both more trustworthy and harder to copy. It's our moat.

Do this

  • Answer the question in the first two sentences, then earn the rest of the read.
  • Use real names, real rands, real places. "R350 a month" beats "affordable".
  • Quote actual founders, lenders and officials — first-hand voices can't be summarised away.
  • Be specific to South Africa: CIPC, SARS, B-BBEE, SEDA, the actual forms.
  • Write short sentences. Take a position and back it.

Not this

  • Don't open with throat-clearing ("In today's fast-paced business world…").
  • Don't pad with synonyms and lists that say nothing.
  • Don't use the corporate-AI vocabulary below — it's a tell.
  • Don't write generic advice that fits any country. That's what AI does best.
  • Don't end with a hollow "In conclusion" summary. Stop when you're done.

The five-second test for every article

Section 07

From resources to membership: the pivot

Some of what looks like decline is a change we made on purpose. In February 2026 we changed what SME South Africa fundamentally is.

Before — resource platform

  • Free templates and tools, open to all.
  • Measured in leads and newsletter sign-ups.
  • Dozens of lead-capture forms.

After — membership platform

  • Members create an account and log in.
  • Measured in sign-ups, activation and retention.
  • Lead forms retired in favour of joining.
The most important point in this report
When the model changed, the old metrics fell by design — form submissions are down 54.6% largely because we switched the forms off. Meanwhile a new metric started climbing: 2,364 members in four months. Judging the new platform on the old scoreboard tells the wrong story.

The pivot is the right call — membership is the more durable business — but it has real costs we should manage: gated content can't rank, relaunches briefly unsettle Google, and every retired page was a door into the site. Keep enough on the open web to attract; reserve the depth for members.
